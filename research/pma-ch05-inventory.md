Entries are brief statement paraphrases only; no source proof text is reproduced.

Unless otherwise stated, functions are real-valued on a nondegenerate real interval (`a<b`). A derivative at an interior point is the ordinary two-sided derivative; at an endpoint of a closed interval it is the corresponding one-sided derivative. Derivatives are finite ordinary limits. Node 5.13 uses extended-real limits from Chapter 4, and 5.16–5.19 explicitly extend the codomain to complex or Euclidean spaces. All monotonicity statements are non-strict.

## Numbered nodes

- **PMA-5.1** (definition, pp.103–104) — For `f:[a,b]→ℝ`, the derivative at `x∈[a,b]` is the limit of `(f(t)−f(x))/(t−x)` as interior domain points `t≠x` approach `x`; differentiability at `x` means this finite limit exists, and differentiability on a set means it exists at every point of that set; right and left derivatives use the corresponding one-sided limits, giving the endpoint derivatives at `a` and `b`, whereas a function defined only on `(a,b)` has no endpoint derivatives. Deps: 4.1–4.2, 4.25.
- **PMA-5.2** (theorem, p.104) — Differentiability at any point of `[a,b]`, including its endpoints with one-sided derivatives, implies continuity there relative to the interval. Deps: 4.4–4.6, 5.1.
- **PMA-5.3** (theorem, pp.104–105) — If `f,g` are differentiable at a domain point, then `(f+g)'=f'+g'`, `(fg)'=f'g+fg'`, and `(f/g)'=(gf'−fg')/g²` when `g` is nonzero at that point, with the quotient defined on the nonzero-denominator domain. Deps: 4.3–4.4, 5.1–5.2.
- **PMA-5.4** (examples, p.105) — Constants have derivative zero and the identity has derivative one; for every integer `n`, the power `x^n` has derivative `n x^(n−1)`, with negative powers restricted to `x≠0` and the constant case interpreted separately at zero; every real polynomial is differentiable everywhere, and every real rational function is differentiable wherever its denominator is nonzero. Deps: 5.3.
- **PMA-5.5** (theorem, p.105) — If `f` is continuous on `[a,b]` and differentiable at `x∈[a,b]`, and `g` is defined on an interval containing `f([a,b])` and differentiable at `f(x)`, then `g∘f` is differentiable at `x` with derivative `g'(f(x))f'(x)`, with the appropriate relative or one-sided derivatives at interval endpoints. Deps: 4.7, 5.1–5.3.
- **PMA-5.6** (examples, p.106) — (a) `f(x)=x sin(1/x)` for `x≠0` and `f(0)=0` is continuous, has derivative `sin(1/x)−cos(1/x)/x` away from zero, and is not differentiable at zero; (b) `h(x)=x² sin(1/x)` for `x≠0` and `h(0)=0` is differentiable everywhere, with derivative `2x sin(1/x)−cos(1/x)` away from zero and `h'(0)=0`, but its derivative is discontinuous at zero. Deps: 4.27(d), 5.1–5.5; trigonometric derivative facts may use pinned Mathlib and are discussed in Chapter 8.
- **PMA-5.7** (definition, p.107) — A real function on a metric space has a local maximum at `p` iff some `δ>0` gives `f(q)≤f(p)` whenever `d(p,q)<δ`; local minimum reverses the inequality. Deps: 2.18.
- **PMA-5.8** (theorem, p.107) — A differentiable real function has derivative zero at any interior point at which it has a local maximum or local minimum. Deps: 5.1, 5.7.
- **PMA-5.9** (theorem, p.107) — For real `f,g` continuous on `[a,b]` and differentiable on `(a,b)`, some `x∈(a,b)` satisfies `(f(b)−f(a))g'(x)=(g(b)−g(a))f'(x)`; no endpoint differentiability is required. Deps: 4.16, 5.3, 5.8.
- **PMA-5.10** (theorem, p.108) — A real function continuous on `[a,b]` and differentiable on `(a,b)` satisfies `f(b)−f(a)=(b−a)f'(x)` for some `x∈(a,b)`. Deps: 5.4, 5.9.
- **PMA-5.11** (theorem, p.108) — On `(a,b)`, an everywhere differentiable real function is increasing when all derivatives are nonnegative, constant when all are zero, and decreasing when all are nonpositive. Deps: 5.10.
- **PMA-5.12** (theorem/corollary, pp.108–109) — If a real function is differentiable on `[a,b]`, every value strictly between its endpoint derivatives, in either ordering, is attained by the derivative at an interior point; its derivative has no simple discontinuities, although second-kind discontinuities can occur. Deps: 4.26, 5.3, 5.7–5.8; see the endpoint convention below.
- **PMA-5.13** (theorem, pp.109–110) — For real differentiable `f,g` on `(a,b)` with extended endpoints `−∞≤a<b≤+∞` and `g'≠0` throughout, if `f'/g'→A∈[-∞,+∞]` at an endpoint and either `f,g→0` there or `g→+∞` or `g→−∞` there, then `f/g→A`; the left endpoint is approached from the right and the right endpoint from the left, including approaches at either infinity, with no limit assumption on `f` in the infinite-denominator cases. Deps: 4.33–4.34, 5.2, 5.9.
- **PMA-5.14** (definition, p.110) — Higher derivatives are obtained recursively by differentiating the preceding derivative, with `f^(0)=f` and `f^(n)` the derivative of order `n`; existence of `f^(n)(x)` requires `f^(n−1)` to exist near `x` and be differentiable at `x`, using a one-sided neighborhood at an interval endpoint, and consequently every lower predecessor has the required local existence and differentiability. Deps: 5.1.
- **PMA-5.15** (theorem/consequence, pp.110–111) — For `n≥1`, if `f^(n−1)` is continuous on `[a,b]` and `f^(n)` exists on `(a,b)`, then for distinct `α,β∈[a,b]` in either order, the polynomial `P(t)=Σ_{k=0}^{n−1} f^(k)(α)(t−α)^k/k!` satisfies `f(β)=P(β)+f^(n)(x)(β−α)^n/n!` at some `x` strictly between `α` and `β`; `n=1` is the mean value theorem, and a bound `|f^(n)|≤M` between them gives `|f(β)−P(β)|≤M|β−α|^n/n!`. Deps: 5.3–5.4, 5.8–5.10, 5.14.
- **PMA-5.16** (remarks, pp.111–112) — The difference-quotient definition extends to complex and Euclidean-valued functions, with the vector limit taken in norm; complex differentiability is equivalent to differentiability of both real and imaginary parts, with derivative `f₁'+i f₂'`, and Euclidean differentiability is equivalent to differentiability of every coordinate, with the coordinatewise derivative; differentiability still implies continuity, complex sum/product/quotient rules remain valid, and Euclidean sum and inner-product rules give `(f+g)'=f'+g'` and `(f·g)'=f'·g+f·g'`. Deps: 3.3–3.4, 4.10, 5.1–5.3.
- **PMA-5.17** (example, p.112) — The complex function `f(x)=cos x+i sin x=exp(ix)` satisfies `f(2π)=f(0)=1` and `f'(x)=i exp(ix)` with `|f'(x)|=1` everywhere; hence no interior point satisfies the real mean value theorem's equality on `[0,2π]`. Deps: 5.10, 5.16; trigonometric and complex-exponential derivatives may use pinned Mathlib and are discussed in Chapter 8.
- **PMA-5.18** (example, pp.112–113) — On `(0,1)`, complex `f(x)=x` and `g(x)=x+x² exp(i/x²)` both tend to zero at `0+`, with nonzero `g,g'`; `f'=1` and `g'=1+(2x−2i/x)exp(i/x²)`, with `|g'|≥|2x−2i/x|−1≥2/x−1` and `|f'/g'|≤x/(2−x)`; nevertheless `f/g→1` while `f'/g'→0`, so the complex version of 5.13 fails. Deps: 4.4, 5.4–5.5, 5.13, 5.16.
- **PMA-5.19** (theorem/consequence, p.113) — A continuous Euclidean-valued map on `[a,b]` that is differentiable on `(a,b)` satisfies `‖f(b)−f(a)‖≤(b−a)‖f'(x)‖` for some `x∈(a,b)`; consequently a uniform bound `‖f'‖≤M` gives `‖f(b)−f(a)‖≤(b−a)M`, including the derivative-norm supremum bound when that supremum is finite. Deps: 1.37, 5.10, 5.16.

## Fidelity conventions and attached consequences

- Nondegenerate intervals are necessary for derivative uniqueness and for interior-point conclusions. The derivative limit in 5.1 uses `(a,b)\{x}` and the domain value `f(x)`; it must not be weakened to a vacuous limit on an isolated point. For `x∈[a,b]`, this has the `HasDerivWithinAt` reference on `[a,b]`; for interior points it agrees with `HasDerivAt`. Endpoint values and the appropriate one-sided derivatives must be retained.
- Source 5.2 explicitly includes `x∈[a,b]`, despite an OCR form resembling `(a,b]`. Node 5.3 uses nonzero denominator at the differentiation point and a locally restricted quotient domain, not a global nonvanishing premise. Node 5.5 allows the outer function to be defined only on an interval containing the inner range; a global `HasDerivAt g` premise could unnecessarily narrow its endpoint cases.
- Integer powers in 5.4 include negative exponents, with domain `x≠0`; the `n=0` constant derivative is zero even at `x=0`, where the written intermediate factor `x^(n−1)` should not introduce an undefined negative power.
- Local extrema in 5.7 use non-strict inequalities; 5.8 requires an interior point. Differentiability only on the open interval is sufficient in 5.9–5.10; the functions are continuous on the closed interval, but no endpoint derivatives are required.
- The attached corollary in 5.12 rules out simple discontinuities of a derivative, not all discontinuities. The Chapter 4 two-sided first-kind classification concerns interior points; at an endpoint, any finite one-sided limit of the derivative agrees with its existing one-sided endpoint derivative, so an endpoint jump is likewise impossible. Example 5.6(b) supplies the second-kind possibility.
- Node 5.13 is a real-valued theorem with an arbitrary extended-real target, not just a finite zero-over-zero rule. It includes both endpoint directions, unbounded intervals, both signs of an infinite denominator, and no numerator-limit requirement in the infinite-denominator cases. The assumptions imply the denominator is eventually nonzero; reference statements must not replace this with an unnecessary global denominator-nonzero assumption or silently use totalized division at a source-forbidden point.
- The recursive existence condition in 5.14 is substantive: a library's totalized `deriv` can be iterated even for nondifferentiable functions, so its iteration alone is not the source's higher-derivative existence predicate. Node 5.15 assumes continuity only through order `n−1`; requiring continuity of the nth derivative would strengthen its hypotheses. Both orders of `α,β` and the sign of `(β−α)^n` must be preserved.
- The complex derivative in 5.16–5.18 has a **real input variable**, so the appropriate reference is `HasDerivAt` for maps `ℝ→ℂ`, not complex differentiability of maps `ℂ→ℂ`. The vector difference quotient uses real scalar division and the Euclidean norm. The inner-product rule is the real Euclidean bilinear rule.
- The precise phase in 5.18 is `i/x²`, not `i/x`; its derivative has the term `−2i/x`. The norm estimates are included as mathematical assertions of the worked example, without reproducing their derivation. Node 5.19 is an existential norm inequality; it must not be replaced by a vector equality or a bound assuming a pre-existing supremum of derivative norms. The nearby displayed supremum bound is folded as a consequence: a finite supremum uses boundedness of the derivative-norm range, while an unbounded range has extended supremum `+∞` and makes that bound vacuous.

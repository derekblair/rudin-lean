# PMA Chapter 6 inventory — The Riemann–Stieltjes Integral

Source: the local third-edition book, printed pp.120–137 (PDF pp.129–146). All 27 numbered main-text items are included. Exercises begin on printed p.138 and are excluded. This inventory contains brief mathematical paraphrases, not the source's proofs or exposition. Independent extraction and comparison accepted this full scope; see `evidence/ch06-source-independent-review.md`. The mathematical inventory is unchanged from its accepted hash; this sentence only updates review status.

Unless stated otherwise, `a≤b`, `f:[a,b]→ℝ` is bounded, and `α:[a,b]→ℝ` is nondecreasing. A partition is a finite ordered list from `a` to `b`; repeated points are permitted. Cell suprema and infima use **closed** subintervals. No right-continuity or global continuity of `α` is implicit. `R(α)` includes boundedness and equality of lower and upper Darboux integrals. Ordinary `R` is the specialization `α=id`. Derivative clauses use nondegenerate intervals `a<b`; finite derivatives at endpoints use the appropriate one-sided interval derivative. Ordered degenerate intervals remain admitted for the integration definitions and non-derivative identities.

## Numbered nodes

- **PMA-6.1** (definition, pp.120–121) — Define finite nondecreasing partitions, cell widths, cell supremum/infimum, upper/lower Riemann sums, upper/lower integrals as their infimum/supremum over all partitions, and Riemann integrability and its common value. Include the bounds `m(b−a)≤L≤U≤M(b−a)` when `m≤f≤M`, which ensure finite upper/lower integrals. Deps: Chapter 1 completeness, finite sums, bounded functions.
- **PMA-6.2** (definition, p.122) — Replace cell width by `α(xᵢ)−α(xᵢ₋₁)` for an arbitrary nondecreasing integrator. Define weighted sums, upper/lower Stieltjes integrals, integrability, and integral value. Weights are nonnegative; boundedness gives finite sums and integral bounds. With `α=id` these coincide with 6.1. Deps: 6.1.
- **PMA-6.3** (definition, p.123) — A refinement contains every partition point; the common refinement has the union of their point sets. Repetition is immaterial to sums. Establish availability of a common refinement. Deps: 6.1.
- **PMA-6.4** (theorem, p.123) — Refinement increases the lower weighted sum and decreases the upper weighted sum. Deps: 6.2–6.3.
- **PMA-6.5** (theorem, p.124) — The lower Stieltjes integral is at most the upper Stieltjes integral. Deps: 6.4 and a common refinement.
- **PMA-6.6** (theorem, p.124) — A bounded function is Stieltjes integrable iff for each positive `ε` a partition has upper minus lower sum less than `ε`. Deps: 6.4–6.5, completeness.
- **PMA-6.7** (theorem, p.125) — If a partition's gap is below `ε`, the same holds for every refinement; any two choices of tags in the closed cells have sum `Σ |f(sᵢ)−f(tᵢ)| Δαᵢ<ε`; if `f` is integrable, every tagged sum on that partition differs from the integral by less than `ε`. Deps: 6.4–6.6.
- **PMA-6.8** (theorem, p.125) — Every function continuous on the closed interval is Stieltjes integrable for every nondecreasing `α`. Deps: 4.19 uniform continuity, 6.6.
- **PMA-6.9** (theorem, p.126) — A monotone real function is Stieltjes integrable whenever `α` is continuous on the closed interval. Both directions of monotonicity are included. Deps: 6.6, uniform continuity of `α`.
- **PMA-6.10** (theorem, p.126) — A bounded function with finitely many discontinuities is Stieltjes integrable if `α` is continuous at each discontinuity of `f`, including relative continuity at interval endpoints. Include the attached caution that a shared discontinuity of `f` and `α` can destroy integrability. Deps: 6.6 and uniform continuity on the complementary compact pieces.
- **PMA-6.11** (theorem, p.127) — If `f∈R(α)`, its values lie in `[m,M]`, and `φ` is continuous there, then `φ∘f∈R(α)`. Deps: 6.6–6.7, uniform continuity.
- **PMA-6.12** (theorem, p.128) — Integrability and integrals respect addition of functions and arbitrary real scalar multiplication; pointwise comparison implies integral comparison; restriction to `[a,c]` and `[c,b]` preserves integrability and integrals add for `a<c<b`; `|f|≤M` implies `|∫f dα|≤M(α(b)−α(a))`; integrability with respect to each of two nondecreasing integrators implies integrability with respect to their sum, with additive integral; positive scaling of an integrator scales its integral. Deps: 6.4–6.7.
- **PMA-6.13** (theorem, p.129) — Products of two Stieltjes integrable functions are integrable; `|f|` is integrable and `|∫f dα|≤∫|f| dα`. Deps: 6.11–6.12.
- **PMA-6.14** (definition, p.129) — Define the unit step by `I(x)=0` for `x≤0` and `I(x)=1` for `x>0`. In particular its value at its jump is zero. Deps: real order.
- **PMA-6.15** (theorem, p.130) — For `a<s<b`, bounded `f` continuous at `s` is integrable with respect to `α(x)=I(x−s)`, and its integral is `f(s)`. Deps: 6.6, 6.14.
- **PMA-6.16** (theorem, p.130) — For a summable nonnegative sequence `cₙ` and distinct `sₙ∈(a,b)`, the pointwise series `α(x)=Σcₙ I(x−sₙ)` defines a nondecreasing integrator. For every continuous `f`, the integral is the convergent series `Σcₙf(sₙ)`. Include well-definedness of the integrator and series, with `α(a)=0` and `α(b)=Σcₙ`, not merely equality of totalized expressions. Deps: 6.8, 6.12, 6.14–6.15, Chapter 3 series.
- **PMA-6.17** (theorem, p.131) — If nondecreasing `α` is differentiable on `[a,b]` and `α'∈R`, then for bounded `f`, `f∈R(α)` iff `fα'∈R`; when integrable their integrals agree. Do not require continuity of `α'`. Deps: 5.10, 6.6–6.7.
- **PMA-6.18** (remark/example, p.132) — The preceding reduction formulas include the quadratic moment on `[0,1]`: for a nondecreasing mass function with Riemann integrable derivative `ρ`, `∫x² dm=∫x²ρ(x) dx`; for the atomic integrator in 6.16 it equals `Σcₙsₙ²`, with the finite atomic version as a specialization. Continuous nondecreasing mass functions not everywhere differentiable also admit this quadratic moment; the two reduction formulas do not exhaust the admissible cases. Deps: 6.8, 6.16–6.17.
- **PMA-6.19** (theorem/consequence, pp.132–133) — If `φ:[A,B]→[a,b]` is continuous, strictly increasing and onto, `f∈R(α)` implies `f∘φ∈R(α∘φ)` and the two integrals agree. In the ordinary-integral case, when `φ'` exists and is Riemann integrable, obtain `∫ₐᵇf = ∫ᴬᴮ(f∘φ)φ'`. Deps: 6.1–6.2, 6.17.
- **PMA-6.20** (theorem, p.133) — For `f∈R[a,b]`, its indefinite integral `F(x)=∫ₐˣf` is continuous on `[a,b]` (indeed uniformly continuous); at every relative continuity point of `f`, `F` has derivative `f(x)`, including the appropriate endpoint derivative. Deps: 6.12, 5.1.
- **PMA-6.21** (theorem, p.134) — If `f∈R[a,b]` and a function `F` is differentiable on `[a,b]` with derivative `f`, then `∫ₐᵇf=F(b)−F(a)`. Deps: 5.10, 6.7.
- **PMA-6.22** (theorem, p.134) — If `F'=f∈R` and `G'=g∈R` on the closed interval, then `∫F g = F(b)G(b)−F(a)G(a)−∫f G`, with both products integrable. Deps: 5.2–5.3, 6.8, 6.13, 6.21.
- **PMA-6.23** (definition/consequences, p.135) — For a finite-dimensional real vector function, Stieltjes integrability means integrability of every coordinate; define its integral coordinatewise. Complex integration is the corresponding two-coordinate specialization. Carry over function linearity, interval additivity, integrator linearity, the full derivative-density integrability equivalence and identity, and indefinite-integral continuity/derivative coordinatewise. Deps: 6.2, 6.12, 6.17, 6.20–6.21, 5.16.
- **PMA-6.24** (theorem, p.135) — If a vector function `f` is coordinatewise Riemann integrable and a vector function `F` has derivative `f` on the interval, its integral is `F(b)−F(a)`. Deps: 6.21, 6.23, 5.16.
- **PMA-6.25** (theorem, p.135) — The Euclidean norm of a coordinatewise Stieltjes integrable vector function is integrable, and the norm of the vector integral is at most the integral of its norm. Include the equivalent complex absolute-value specialization. Deps: 6.11–6.13, 6.23, Cauchy–Schwarz.
- **PMA-6.26** (definition, p.136) — A curve is a continuous map of a closed real interval into Euclidean space; an arc is such a map injective on its domain; a closed curve has equal endpoint values. Define polygonal length for a partition and curve length as the supremum over partitions, allowing `+∞`; rectifiability means finite length. The parameterized map, rather than only its range, determines length. Deps: 6.1, metric distance, completeness.
- **PMA-6.27** (theorem, p.137) — A curve with continuous derivative on `[a,b]`, using one-sided derivatives at endpoints, is rectifiable and its length is the ordinary Riemann integral of the norm of its derivative. Deps: 5.19, 6.7, 6.8, 6.24–6.26.

## Fidelity gates

- Retain arbitrary nondecreasing integrators and the closed-cell convention. Right-continuous Stieltjes measures and a uniform-mesh Riemann-sum limit are not definitionally interchangeable with these Darboux integrals; either bridge requires a proof with sufficient hypotheses.
- A step's value at the jump matters. In 6.14 it is the left-continuous convention. Continuous integrands in 6.15–6.16 make the stated point-mass formulas valid for this convention.
- In 6.7(b) the absolute value is inside the finite sum. In 6.12(d) it bounds the absolute value of the integral.
- No derivative continuity is assumed in 6.17, 6.19's weighted consequence, 6.21, or 6.22. Continuity of the derivative is specifically assumed in 6.27.
- Equivalence/interpretation lemmas must expose the source's boundedness, partition, order, endpoint, and finite-dimensional semantics. A totalized integral or derivative alone does not assert its existence.
- Numbered remarks and attached consequences are included; where an accepted node has unproved conjuncts, it is not counted complete.

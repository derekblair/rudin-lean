# PMA Chapter 10 inventory - Integration of Differential Forms

Source: authorized local third-edition PDF, printed pp.245-288 (PDF pp.254-297). Every source page was visually inspected from an in-memory rendering. There are **51 consecutive numbered items, PMA-10.1 through PMA-10.51**. Exercises begin on printed p.288 after 10.51 and are excluded. Numbered definitions, examples, discussions, and attached assertions are included. This is an inventory, not an implementation-completion claim.

## Integration and local decomposition

- **PMA-10.1** - definition, pp.245-246: Integrate a continuous real function on a closed coordinate cell by successive ordinary one-variable Riemann integrals, initially in last-coordinate-first order. All slice integrals exist and the intermediate functions are continuous on the lower-dimensional cells. Side intervals may be degenerate. The native Chapter 6 integral must remain identifiable.
- **PMA-10.2** - theorem, p.246: For a continuous real function on a cell, every permutation of the order of successive coordinate integrations exists and gives the same value.
- **PMA-10.3** - definition/consequence, pp.246-247: The support of a real or complex function is the closure of its nonzero set. Define the whole-space integral of a continuous function with compact support using any containing cell; the value does not depend on that cell. Complex integration may be implemented through real and imaginary components, with the corresponding existence and independence statements.
- **PMA-10.4** - example, pp.247-248: For the standard closed simplex `Q_k={x: all x_i>=0 and sum x_i<=1}`, extend a continuous real function by zero to the unit cell. Every order of successive Riemann integration exists and gives the same value, defining its simplex integral. The extension need not be continuous on the simplex boundary.
- **PMA-10.5** - definition/consequences, p.248: A primitive map changes only one coordinate, replacing `x_m` by `g(x)`. If `g` is differentiable at a point, the primitive map is differentiable there; row `m` of its derivative matrix is the row of partials of `g`, and all other rows are those of the identity. Its Jacobian is `D_m g`, and its derivative is invertible exactly when that partial is nonzero.
- **PMA-10.6** - definition/consequences, p.249: A flip exchanges two distinct standard coordinate vectors and fixes the others, equivalently exchanging those two coordinates. The prefix projection `P_m` retains the first `m` coordinates; `P_0=0`, `P_n=id`, its range is spanned by the first `m` coordinate vectors, and its kernel by the remaining vectors.
- **PMA-10.7** - theorem, pp.249-250: In positive dimension, a C1 map on an open neighborhood of zero, fixing zero and with invertible derivative there, factors on a smaller neighborhood as `B_1 ... B_(n-1) G_n ... G_1`. There are exactly `n` primitive factors, each C1 near zero, fixing zero, and with invertible derivative there; each of the `n-1` other factors is a flip or identity. Composition acts rightmost first. Include the neighborhood's containment in the original domain.
- **PMA-10.8** - theorem/consequence, p.251: For a compact set and any open cover, there is a finite continuous partition: functions globally between zero and one, each support contained in its assigned cover member, and sum one on the compact set. Consequently a continuous function supported there is globally the sum of its products with the partition functions; each product is continuous and supported in the assigned member. Support means the closed support, not just the nonzero set.
- **PMA-10.9** - theorem/consequence, pp.252-253: An injective C1 map on an open subset of equal-dimensional Euclidean space, with nowhere-zero Jacobian, changes the whole-space integral of a continuous function compactly supported in its image by the factor `abs Jacobian`. The transformed function is understood as zero outside the map's domain and is continuous with compact support inside that domain. Include the inverse's continuity. An arbitrary global extension of the map must not contribute extraneous integral outside the domain.

## Differential forms and their algebra

Unnumbered convention on p.253: C1 or C2 regularity on a compact parameter set means extension to a map of that regularity on an open neighborhood. Boundary derivatives must respect this convention.

- **PMA-10.10** - definition, p.254: A k-surface in an open Euclidean set is a C1 parametrization from a compact k-cell or standard k-simplex into that set. It is the parametrized map, not merely its image. Neither injectivity nor immersion is required. For k=1 these are C1 curves.
- **PMA-10.11** - definition, p.254: A positive-degree k-form has continuous real coefficients in an all-ordered-coordinate-tuple expression. Its integral over a surface is the native parameter-domain integral of the sum of coefficient values times the corresponding derivative minors. C1/C2 forms have coefficients of that regularity. A zero-form is a continuous real function. An alternating-map implementation requires an explicit bridge to these coefficient and integral formulas.
- **PMA-10.12** - examples, pp.254-255: (a) The integral of `x dy+y dx` along a C1 curve is the endpoint difference of `xy`, hence is zero on a closed curve. (b) On the positively parametrized ellipse `(a cos t,b sin t)`, with positive `a,b`, the integrals of `x dy` and `y dx` are respectively `pi*a*b` and its negative. (c) The spherical-coordinate parametrization `(r sin(theta) cos(phi),r sin(theta) sin(phi),r cos(theta))` on `[0,1]x[0,pi]x[0,2pi]` has Jacobian `r^2 sin(theta)` and volume-form integral `4*pi/3`; it covers the closed unit ball and is injective on the parameter interior.
- **PMA-10.13** - elementary properties, p.256: Equality of forms means equality of their integrals over every admissible surface; zero means all these integrals vanish. Integration respects scalar multiplication, addition, and negation. Swapping two coordinate indices changes sign, a repeated index gives zero, and every k-form in dimension n vanishes for k>n. Do not replace the asserted functional equality with a definition of pointwise tensor equality without a proved bridge.
- **PMA-10.14** - basic forms, p.257: Increasing k-tuples give the basic forms, of which there are `choose(n,k)`. Sorting a tuple of distinct indices gives its sign times the basic form; repetitions give zero. Every expression has a standard expansion over increasing tuples. Include the displayed reorderings `(1,5,3,2)` with negative sign and `(4,2,3)` with positive sign, and the displayed two-form's standard coefficients `1-x_1` and `x_2+x_3`. The Chapter 9 tuple-sign convention must agree with this one.
- **PMA-10.15** - theorem, p.258: If a standard expansion integrates to zero over every admissible k-surface in the open set, each standard coefficient is zero at every point. This is separation by actual integrals, not merely uniqueness of coordinates in an alternating tensor.
- **PMA-10.16** - products of basic forms, pp.258-259: Wedge concatenates coordinate lists. A shared index gives zero; otherwise sorting contributes the parity of the cross-inversions. Basic wedge multiplication is associative, including when indices repeat.
- **PMA-10.17** - multiplication, pp.259-260: Extend wedge by coefficient multiplication and finite distributivity. It is distributive in both arguments and associative; multiplication by a zero-form is ordinary coefficient multiplication. Preserve the signs dictated by exchanging factors of degrees k and m.
- **PMA-10.18** - differentiation, p.260: Exterior differentiation takes a C1 k-form to a continuous (k+1)-form. For a scalar function, `df=sum_i D_i f dx_i`; for a standard form, differentiate each coefficient and wedge its differential on the left of the basic form.
- **PMA-10.19** - example/consequence, pp.260-261: The integral of `df` on a C1 curve is the difference of endpoint values, so it depends only on the endpoints. The form `x dy` is not globally an exact form; its nonzero closed-curve integral supplies a witness.
- **PMA-10.20** - theorem, pp.261-262: The exterior derivative satisfies the graded product rule `d(omega wedge lambda)=d omega wedge lambda+(-1)^k omega wedge d lambda` for C1 forms, and `d(d omega)=0` for C2 forms. Include zero-degree cases.

## Pullbacks and oriented chains

- **PMA-10.21** - definition, p.262: For any C1 map between open Euclidean sets, define the pullback of a form by composition of its coefficients and wedging the differentials of the map's coordinate functions. Injectivity is not assumed.
- **PMA-10.22** - theorem, p.263: Pullback preserves addition and wedge products. It commutes with exterior differentiation when the form is C1 and the map is C2.
- **PMA-10.23** - theorem, p.264: Pullback by a composite of C1 maps equals successive pullbacks in contravariant order.
- **PMA-10.24** - theorem, pp.264-265: Integrating a form over a parametrized surface equals integrating its pullback over the identity parametrization of the parameter domain. Use the neighborhood-extension convention at the domain boundary.
- **PMA-10.25** - theorem, pp.265-266: For a C1 map T and a surface Phi, integration over `T after Phi` equals integration of the pullback by T over Phi. No injectivity of T or Phi is required.
- **PMA-10.26** - affine simplices, pp.266-267: An affine map is determined by its value at zero and its values at standard basis vectors. An oriented k-simplex with vertices `p_0,...,p_k` is the map `u -> p_0+sum_i u_i*(p_i-p_0)` on the standard simplex; degenerate simplices are allowed. Vertex permutations give same or opposite orientation according to their sign, as integration equivalence rather than literal equality of parametrizations. Full-dimensional nondegenerate orientation agrees with the sign of the edge determinant. A signed zero-simplex integrates a function to its signed value at the point.
- **PMA-10.27** - theorem, pp.267-268: Permuting the vertices of an oriented affine simplex multiplies every form integral by the permutation sign. Include the zero-dimensional case.
- **PMA-10.28** - affine chains, pp.268-269: A chain is a finite collection of oriented simplices with multiplicities; its integral is the sum. Addition, negatives, and zero are interpreted through their integration functionals, not addition of parametrizing maps.
- **PMA-10.29** - boundaries, p.269: The boundary of a positive-dimensional affine simplex is the alternating sum of the faces obtained by deleting each vertex, with sign `(-1)^j` for zero-based vertex j. Include the triangle orientation and the explicit standard face parametrizations; the face deleting vertex zero has a different base vertex.
- **PMA-10.30** - differentiable simplices/chains, pp.269-270: Compose affine simplices with a C2 map on an open neighborhood to obtain C2 simplices and finite C2 chains. The map need not be injective. Integrals and pushforwards are additive, the boundary commutes with this pushforward, and the convention agrees with the affine boundary when the map is affine.
- **PMA-10.31** - positively oriented boundaries, pp.270-271: Orient the boundary of the standard full-dimensional simplex by its alternating faces. An injective C2 parametrization with positive interior Jacobian induces the positive boundary of its image. The resulting boundary integrals are independent of the positive parametrization, for every continuous form of boundary degree. For finite unions with disjoint interiors, add the boundaries. Include the unit square's triangulation into two triangles, cancellation of the diagonal, and its four oriented outer edges; rectangular parametrizations inherit that boundary.
- **PMA-10.32** - example, p.272: The spherical surface `(sin u cos v,sin u sin v,cos u)` on `[0,pi]x[0,2pi]` has zero boundary as an integration functional on continuous one-forms: two edges are constant at the poles and the two meridians cancel.
- **PMA-10.33** - Stokes theorem, pp.272-275: The integral of `d omega` over any C2 k-chain in an open set equals the integral of the C1 (k-1)-form omega over its boundary, for k>=1. Preserve general dimension, arbitrary finite chains, and the native parameter integral. The one-dimensional fundamental theorem and the later classical vector-calculus formulas are instances.

## Closed and exact forms

- **PMA-10.34** - definition/consequence, p.275: A positive-degree form is exact if it is the exterior derivative of a form of one lower degree, and a C1 form is closed if its derivative is zero. Every exact form that is C1 is closed. Exactness must not be silently strengthened to the existence of a C2 primitive; the ordinary primitive needs enough regularity to define its exterior derivative.
- **PMA-10.35** - remarks, pp.275-276: (a) A C1 one-form is closed exactly when the cross partials of its coefficients agree, and exact exactly when these coefficients are the partials of a global potential. (b) Exact-form integrals over C2 chains depend only on the boundary; in particular they vanish on cycles. (c) Closed-form integrals vanish over boundaries of C2 chains. (d) Applying the boundary operator twice gives zero, with the chapter's integration-functional meaning.
- **PMA-10.36** - example, p.277: On the punctured plane, `(x dy-y dx)/(x^2+y^2)` is closed and integrates to `2*pi` on every positively parametrized circle centered at zero. It is not exact, and such a circle is not the boundary of any C2 two-chain in the punctured plane.
- **PMA-10.37** - example, p.277: On punctured three-space, `(x dy wedge dz+y dz wedge dx+z dx wedge dy)/(x^2+y^2+z^2)^(3/2)` is closed and integrates to `4*pi` over the sphere of 10.32. It is not exact, and this sphere is not the boundary of any C2 three-chain in that domain.
- **PMA-10.38** - theorem, p.278: On an arbitrary convex open subset of n-space, a C1 function whose coordinate partials after index p vanish has a global C1 primitive in coordinate p whose later partials also vanish, for `1<=p<=n`. The domain need not be a product and no common fixed integration basepoint is assumed.
- **PMA-10.39** - Poincare lemma, pp.278-279: Every closed C1 k-form on a convex open set, for k>=1, is exact. The primitive exists globally on the set and has the regularity needed for exterior differentiation. Neither a potential nor total exactness may be inserted as a premise.
- **PMA-10.40** - theorem/corollary, p.280: If every closed k-form is exact on an open set, the same holds on an open set related to it by a C2 bijection with C2 inverse. In particular, a C2-diffeomorphic image of a convex open set has this property. Preserve the two-sided regularity of the change of coordinates.
- **PMA-10.41** - remark, p.280: Cells admit finite triangulations and can therefore be treated as chains. Include the asserted compatibility with the integral and boundary conventions; do not count a prose comment alone as a formalized triangulation assertion. References to further topology and exercises add no separate nodes.

## Vector analysis

- **PMA-10.42** - vector fields, p.281: A continuous field in three-space corresponds to the one-form `F_1 dx+F_2 dy+F_3 dz` and to the two-form `F_1 dy wedge dz+F_2 dz wedge dx+F_3 dx wedge dy`; conversely every one-form or two-form has such a representation. Define the gradient of a C1 scalar function and the curl and divergence of a C1 field by their coordinate partial formulas.
- **PMA-10.43** - theorem, pp.281-282: The curl of the gradient of a C2 scalar function and the divergence of the curl of a C2 field vanish. On an open set C2-equivalent to a convex set, a C1 curl-free field has a C2 scalar potential, and a C1 divergence-free field has a C2 vector potential. Preserve both potential regularities and the form/derivative identifications used to transfer these statements. The two-prime output superscripts were checked against the single-prime input superscript in an enlarged rendering.
- **PMA-10.44** - volume elements, p.282: The basic top-degree form is the volume element. For a positively oriented injective C1 full-dimensional parametrization, its form integral with a continuous density equals the ordinary integral of that density over the image, by change of variables. Density one gives volume; dimension two gives the area notation. A semantic bridge to the native integral is required.
- **PMA-10.45** - Green theorem/consequence, pp.282-283: For a closed planar region with positive boundary in the sense of 10.31 and C1 coefficients alpha,beta on a surrounding open set, the boundary integral of `alpha dx+beta dy` equals the region integral of `D_x beta-D_y alpha`. Include the area identities from `alpha=-y,beta=x` and from `alpha=0,beta=x`.
- **PMA-10.46** - area elements, pp.283-285: For a C1 two-surface in three-space, the directed normal N has the three coordinate Jacobian minors as components. Define the area integral of f as the native parameter integral of `f(Phi(u,v))*norm N`, and area by f=1. N is perpendicular to both derivative columns; with rank two, their affine image is the tangent plane, N is nonzero, and the determinant with columns `(T e_1,T e_2,N)` equals `norm N^2>0`. The derivative's unit-square parallelogram has area `norm N`; affine maps scale each parameter rectangle's area by this factor. Rank below two gives N=0 and a degenerate tangent image. General surfaces need not be injective.
- **PMA-10.47** - example, pp.285-286: For `0<a<b`, the toroidal map `(t cos u,(b+t sin u) cos v,(b+t sin u) sin v)` on `[0,a]x[0,2*pi]^2` is injective on the interior and has Jacobian `t*(b+t sin u)`, positive except at t=0. Its solid-torus volume is `2*pi^2*a^2*b`. Its boundary reduces, as a chain functional, to the surface t=a; the other nonconstant face pairs cancel and the t=0 face is degenerate. The surface normal is `a*(b+a sin u)*(cos u,sin u cos v,sin u sin v)`, points outward, and has that positive scalar as norm. The torus area is `4*pi^2*a*b`.
- **PMA-10.48** - integrals of one-forms, pp.286-287: For a C1 curve and a continuous field, the associated one-form integral equals the native parameter integral of the field dotted with velocity, and equivalently of its tangential component times speed. Choose any fixed unit tangent at zero-velocity points; the speed makes that choice immaterial. The arc-length notation abbreviates this parameter formula and does not assume the curve is injective.
- **PMA-10.49** - integrals of two-forms, p.287: The associated two-form integral over a C1 surface equals the parameter integral of the field dotted with its directed normal, equivalently the normal component integrated against area. At zero normal choose an arbitrary unit vector; the norm factor makes the choice immaterial. The normal is attached to the parameter point even for a noninjective surface.
- **PMA-10.50** - classical Stokes formula, pp.287-288: For a C1 vector field and C2 two-surface in an open subset of three-space, the flux of its curl through the surface equals the circulation of the field along the oriented boundary. This must identify the field flux and circulation with the native form integrals, not introduce the desired identity as a definition.
- **PMA-10.51** - divergence theorem, p.288: For a C1 field on an open subset of three-space and a closed region with positive boundary as in 10.31, the region integral of its divergence equals the outward flux through its boundary. Retain the region/boundary admissibility and volume/area interpretations.

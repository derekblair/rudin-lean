## Source conventions

Unless a node explicitly changes codomain or integrator, f is a bounded real-valued function on the closed interval and alpha is a real-valued nondecreasing function there. Alpha is not globally assumed continuous. R(alpha) means Darboux Riemann–Stieltjes integrability; R denotes ordinary Riemann integrability with identity integrator. Upper/lower sums use extrema over closed subintervals. The source's partition display uses **nonstrict inequalities**, so repeated partition points are permitted and their increments contribute zero. Differentiability at endpoints follows Chapter 5's one-sided convention. Do not add a global strict-positivity assumption on integrator increments.

## Numbered nodes

- **PMA-6.1 — Definition, pp.120–121.** A finite ordered partition includes both endpoints; define interval increments, subinterval suprema/infima, upper/lower Riemann sums, upper integral as the infimum of upper sums, and lower integral as the supremum of lower sums. Bounded real functions have finite upper/lower integrals. Equality defines Riemann integrability and its integral. Include the bounds obtained from any global lower/upper bound on f.
- **PMA-6.2 — Definition, pp.122–123.** For nondecreasing alpha, use increments alpha(x_i)-alpha(x_(i-1)) in the upper/lower sums. Equality of upper/lower Stieltjes integrals defines R(alpha) and its integral. The identity integrator recovers ordinary Riemann integration. Integrator continuity is unnecessary; variable-of-integration naming has no mathematical effect.
- **PMA-6.3 — Definition, p.123.** A refinement contains every partition point; the union of two partitions gives their common refinement.
- **PMA-6.4 — Theorem, p.123.** Refinement increases the lower Stieltjes sum and decreases the upper Stieltjes sum.
- **PMA-6.5 — Theorem, p.124.** The lower Stieltjes integral is at most the upper Stieltjes integral. The associated bounds place every lower sum below every upper sum and the two integrals between appropriate lower/upper sums.
- **PMA-6.6 — Theorem, pp.124–125.** Stieltjes integrability is equivalent to the existence, for every positive epsilon, of a partition whose upper-minus-lower sum is less than epsilon.
- **PMA-6.7 — Theorem, p.125.** A partition with upper/lower gap below epsilon passes the same bound to refinements. For any two choices of tags in each closed subinterval, the sum of absolute differences of tagged function values weighted by integrator increments is below epsilon. If f is integrable, each tagged sum differs from its integral by less than epsilon.
- **PMA-6.8 — Theorem, pp.125–126.** A real function continuous on the closed interval is Stieltjes integrable against every nondecreasing alpha there.
- **PMA-6.9 — Theorem, p.126.** A monotone real f is Stieltjes integrable when alpha is both nondecreasing and continuous on the closed interval. Either monotonicity direction for f is allowed.
- **PMA-6.10 — Theorem, pp.126–127.** A bounded f with only finitely many discontinuities is Stieltjes integrable if alpha is continuous at every discontinuity of f. The nearby note records that a common discontinuity can make integrability fail; the referenced exercise itself remains excluded.
- **PMA-6.11 — Theorem, p.127.** If f is Stieltjes integrable with values in a real closed interval [m,M], and phi is continuous on that interval, then phi composed with f is Stieltjes integrable for the same alpha. No monotonicity of phi is assumed.
- **PMA-6.12 — Theorem, pp.128–129.** Include all five parts: linearity and closure under addition/scalar multiplication of integrands; order preservation for integrable integrands; integrability on both subintervals and integral additivity at an interior split point; absolute integral bound from a uniform absolute bound on f; addition of nondecreasing integrators and positive scalar multiplication of an integrator, with corresponding integral identities.
- **PMA-6.13 — Theorem, p.129.** Products of Stieltjes-integrable real functions remain integrable. The absolute value of an integrable f is integrable, and the absolute value of its integral is bounded by the integral of its absolute value.
- **PMA-6.14 — Definition, p.129.** The unit step I is zero on x less than or equal to zero and one on x greater than zero. Its value at zero is essential.
- **PMA-6.15 — Theorem, p.130.** For an interior s, bounded f continuous at s is Stieltjes integrable against alpha(x)=I(x-s), and its integral equals f(s). Continuity away from s is not required.
- **PMA-6.16 — Theorem, pp.130–131.** For a summable sequence of nonnegative coefficients and distinct sample points in the open interval, form alpha by summing the shifted unit steps with those coefficients. A continuous f has Stieltjes integral equal to the corresponding convergent weighted series of its sampled values. The step series is well defined, nondecreasing, zero at the left endpoint, and equals the coefficient sum at the right endpoint. Zero coefficients are allowed; strict positivity is not required.
- **PMA-6.17 — Theorem, pp.131–132.** Suppose alpha is nondecreasing, its derivative exists on the closed interval in the Chapter 5 sense, and that derivative is Riemann integrable. For merely bounded real f, Stieltjes integrability against alpha is equivalent to Riemann integrability of f times alpha's derivative; when they exist, the integrals agree. This is an equivalence, not only a conversion for already Riemann-integrable f.
- **PMA-6.18 — Remark with examples, p.132.** Stieltjes integration unifies weighted-series and density-integral cases. For the unit-length wire's moment functional, the quadratic-position integral against cumulative mass becomes the quadratic-position density integral when mass has a continuous density, and the weighted sum of quadratic positions for point masses. The definition also admits continuous mass functions not everywhere differentiable; do not identify all admissible integrators with either a step sum or a differentiable density.
- **PMA-6.19 — Change-of-variable theorem, pp.132–133.** A strictly increasing continuous map from [A,B] onto [a,b] transports alpha and f by composition; the transported f is integrable against the transported alpha, with equal integrals. Fold the stated special case: for ordinary Riemann f and a reparameterization whose derivative exists and is Riemann integrable, the ordinary substitution formula uses f composed with the map times its derivative. No continuously differentiable or inverse-differentiability premise is required.
- **PMA-6.20 — Theorem, pp.133–134.** For Riemann-integrable f, its integral from the fixed left endpoint to a variable endpoint is continuous on the whole closed interval; wherever f is continuous, this primitive has derivative f, including the appropriate endpoint derivatives. The accompanying argument also establishes uniform continuity, with the usual uniform-bound Lipschitz estimate.
- **PMA-6.21 — Fundamental theorem of calculus, p.134.** If f is Riemann integrable and is the derivative of a differentiable F on the closed interval, its integral is the endpoint difference of F. Continuity of f is not required.
- **PMA-6.22 — Integration by parts, p.134.** For differentiable F and G whose derivatives f and g are Riemann integrable, both products in the integration-by-parts identity are integrable, and the integral of F times g equals the endpoint product difference minus the integral of f times G.
- **PMA-6.23 — Definition and extensions, p.135.** A Euclidean-valued function is Stieltjes integrable exactly when all real coordinate functions are; define its integral coordinatewise. The text explicitly extends parts (a), (c), and (e) of 6.12 and the vector counterparts of 6.17, 6.20, and 6.21. Preserve vector linearity, interval splitting, integrator linearity, density-conversion equivalence, primitive continuity/derivative, and the vector fundamental theorem; 6.24 separately states the last item.
- **PMA-6.24 — Theorem, p.135.** For Euclidean-valued f and F, if f is Riemann integrable and F has derivative f on the closed interval, the vector integral is the endpoint difference of F.
- **PMA-6.25 — Theorem, pp.135–136.** A Euclidean-valued Stieltjes-integrable f has integrable Euclidean norm, and the norm of its vector integral is at most the integral of its norm.
- **PMA-6.26 — Definition, p.136.** A curve is a continuous parameterized map from a closed interval to Euclidean space, an arc is such a map injective on that domain, and a closed curve has matching endpoint values. Its polygonal length is the partition sum of successive chord norms; length is the supremum over all partitions, potentially infinite; rectifiable means finite length. Distinct parameterized curves may have the same image.
- **PMA-6.27 — Theorem, p.137.** A curve with a continuous derivative on the closed parameter interval is rectifiable, and its length equals the Riemann integral of its derivative's Euclidean norm. Endpoint derivatives follow the one-sided convention; injectivity is not required.

## Fidelity pitfalls and matters to settle before typed contracts

1. **Darboux semantics and boundedness.** The source theory is for bounded real functions with a nondecreasing real integrator, before the explicit coordinate extension. A totalized integral or supremum operation must not silently confer integrability or finite extrema without those hypotheses. A direct Darboux-to-library equivalence needs actual proof; a measure integral alone is not the source definition.
2. **Intervals and repeated partition points.** The printed partition relation is nonstrict. Consecutive repeated points contribute zero length and zero integrator increment. Strict partitions may be a proved equivalent representation after removing repeats, but changing the definition without such a bridge loses source coverage. The source does not explicitly impose a<b throughout the definitions; degenerate-interval conventions need to be recorded rather than silently generalized from later derivative nodes.
3. **Discontinuous integrators.** Many fundamental results allow alpha to jump. A library Riemann–Stieltjes integral with a different tagging convention, assumed integrator continuity, or only mesh-based convergence may be narrower than this Darboux definition. Source continuity premises are relative to the closed interval, including its endpoints.
4. **Unit-step orientation.** I(0)=0, so I is left-continuous at its jump. In 6.15 and 6.16 every jump location is strictly interior, and in 6.16 locations are distinct, coefficients are nonnegative, and their sum converges. Do not replace the step with the common right-continuous convention without proving the required integral equivalence.
5. **The physical example's endpoints.** Remark 6.18 describes cumulative mass on [0,x], while the earlier shifted-step series uses I(0)=0. Interior masses agree for continuous integrands, but blindly placing a left-continuous step at the final endpoint omits that endpoint's mass. For any literal endpoint-mass version, use a cumulative-mass convention whose terminal value includes it; a mass at zero contributes zero to the quadratic moment. The source's informal example does not explicitly specify support/endpoints, so the formal statement must document this interpretation rather than invent an unqualified step-series formula at both endpoints.
6. **Derivative conversion and FTC.** In 6.17, integrability of alpha's derivative is sufficient; continuity of that derivative is an extra restriction. In 6.20–6.24, no continuous-integrand premise may replace mere integrability where the source does not require it. Differentiability on a closed interval includes its one-sided endpoints, while ordinary within-interval interior derivatives must not be confused with arbitrary totalized derivative values.
7. **Change of variables.** 6.19 is an order-preserving, continuous, onto reparameterization theorem. Its main Stieltjes identity assumes no derivative at all. Its ordinary substitution consequence adds an integrable derivative, not positivity/nonvanishing of that derivative or differentiability of the inverse.
8. **Vector coverage.** Merely defining coordinatewise integration and proving 6.24–6.25 would omit the explicit extensions attached to 6.23. Preserve Euclidean norms and the real scalar derivative parameter throughout; complex-valued integration may be treated through its real two-coordinate identification.
9. **Curve length.** The object is the parameterized map, not its image set. Length may be infinite before rectifiability is known. A real-valued supremum without a boundedness predicate cannot express nonrectifiable curves faithfully. The main 6.27 theorem requires no injectivity.
